<p style="margin-top:12pt; margin-bottom:0pt; text-indent:4%; font-size:10pt; font-family:Times New Roman" align="justify">On <temporal>March 5, 2024</temporal>, the CFPB published a final rule on late fees. For credit card issuers such as American Express with
more than 1 million open accounts, this rule: (i) decreases the safe harbor dollar amount for credit card late fees that would be considered reasonable and proportional to the costs incurred by credit card issuers for late
payments to $8; (ii) eliminates a higher safe harbor dollar amount for subsequent late payments; and (iii) eliminates the annual inflation adjustments for the late fee safe harbor dollar amounts. The safe harbor dollar amounts
referenced in the rule refer to the amounts that credit card issuers may charge as late fees under the CARD Act without providing a cost justification. Under the CARD Act, as previously implemented, these safe harbor amounts have been subject to
annual adjustment based on changes in the consumer price index, and the safe harbor amounts had been set at $30 for an initial late fee and $41 for subsequent late fees in one of the next six billing cycles. Accordingly, the $8 safe harbor amount on
late fees (and elimination of the annual inflation-based adjustment thereto), represent a significant decrease from the prior safe harbor amounts. </p>
<p style="margin-top:12pt; margin-bottom:0pt; text-indent:4%; font-size:10pt; font-family:Times New Roman" align="justify">In <temporal>May 2024</temporal>, a preliminary injunction was granted staying the effectiveness of the final rule; however, whether the final rule
